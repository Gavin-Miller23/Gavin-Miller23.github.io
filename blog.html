Prompt: When reflecting on my experience with open source software as a user, student, and contributor, I find it easy to say that I have had mostly good experiences. As far as my user experience goes, I find open source software comfortable and easy to use. The thing about being a user of open source software is that I don’t often realize the software is open source. If an error occurs, I never think that it’s because it’s open source, and I don’t often go out of my way to see if the people fixing these issues are company workers or random people fixing issues. As a student, open source software has also been pleasant to use. One example is runestone, where we do our reading. I enjoy this software and I feel like the bug fixes are consistent, especially for being open source. As a student and contributor for the class Open Source Software Engineering, I have also had good experiences. Learning to contribute to open source software was not an easy task. Even while learning new languages and coding within an unknown codebase, most of the code fixes I have made so far are of relatively easy to moderate difficulty. Still, learning to navigate this codebase without knowing where to go to fix issues was a big learning curve. Now, I am much more adept at looking through it as I’ve learned the bcsr codebase and how to navigate it relatively well. Making contributions, while reviewing changes and working with reviewers, has been a pleasant learning experience. Some pros of open source software include the large amount of people who can work on projects, and people who will work for free. Open source allows projects to get many more people working on projects that companies may not be able to handle by themselves, while they are also unpaid, which can be helpful for companies that may not be able to afford the amount of workers they truly need. Some cons include software becoming too reliant on these people with no obligations, and people burning out. Companies can become too reliant on random people helping them, which can lead to a downfall especially if they lean heavily on one person who burns out due to putting so much work in and feeling almost obligated to maintain the software. I believe open source software is an effective model for building critical software infrastructure, despite its flaws. I believe this as it gives the chance to many aspiring computer scientists, as well as those who just have issues they want to fix, to learn and help with something they care about. While open source can lean too heavily on unpaid volunteers and extremely helpful contributors, I believe it is effective due to the large number of people out there who want to help. As seen in this article (Major Companies Leading Open Source in 2026), many large companies such as Red Hat are fully open source, proving that massive software projects can be built through open source contribution. Reflection: So far, I have completed 4 story points, though I will soon have 7 when my next PR is merged. I have completed two tasks with 2 story points assigned, and am close to being done with a 3 SP task. This number is what it is because that is the amount of time I have had to complete tasks. These tasks were about as difficult as I had initially imagined. One blocker/change was the current issue I’m working on, but I made a sub issue for it and solved the problem. The initial learning curve of navigating bcsr definitely halted my progress at first, and team dynamics haven’t been an issue since my smaller issues haven’t needed collaboration, and the reviewing process has gone well.
